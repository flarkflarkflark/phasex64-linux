# v0.1.11 implementation plan and non-hardware status

The RIAA, panel-lifecycle and codec-resync parts below are implemented and have
passed their non-hardware tests. Hardware validation and publication still
require the user's explicit approval.

## 1. RIAA in the capture path

### Scope and controls

Apply RIAA playback equalization independently to capture pairs 1/2 and 3/4,
only while that pair's `In ... Capture Source` enum is `Phono` (WM8776 R21 bit
`0x2`). Pair 3/4 really can select Phono: it is present in
`px_adc_34_sources[]` at `phasex64.c:1480-1486`, and Windows has a separate
3/4 `createRIAA` path at `msnkphsu.sys.c:6403-6407` (decompiled locally with
Ghidra, not distributed).

The implementation adds these default-on boolean controls:

- `Phono RIAA 1/2 Capture Switch`
- `Phono RIAA 3/4 Capture Switch`

The effective condition is `riaa module default/master && pair switch &&
capture source == Phono`. Add an out-of-tree/DKMS module parameter `riaa=1`.
Keeping the parameter makes a raw-capture path available without maintaining a
second driver; for a mainline-oriented build, default to or retain raw capture
and move the DSP to userspace (section 5).

Reset all affected channel state on capture `trigger(START)`, rate change,
capture-source change and RIAA-switch change. A pause/release may preserve
state; a fresh stream must not inherit a previous record's tail. Source and
switch changes must update/reset under `px->cap.lock`. Existing lock order is
safe if control puts keep `px->mutex -> px->cap.lock`; the capture completion
must never take `px->mutex`.

### Coefficients and exact arithmetic

Use the 3180/318/75 us analogue curve and normalize the digital numerator to
0 dB at 1 kHz. The selected one-biquad hybrid maps the three finite analogue
roots with matched-z and chooses the surplus zero by a scalar minimax fit over
20 Hz--20 kHz. Unlike the original 1-kHz-prewarped BLT, it does not force a
zero at Nyquist. Full alternatives and response comparisons are in
`analysis/NOTES.md` and `tests/hw/riaa/design_compare.py`. The final float and
Q30 tables are:

| rate | b0 | b1 | b2 | a1 | a2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 44100 | 0.228910667793636 | -0.175340342942947 | -0.035213056747011 | -1.731979026020091 | 0.733832928312397 |
| 48000 | 0.214818305484235 | -0.168746850591770 | -0.030391314430797 | -1.750935179102223 | 0.752518919515998 |
| 88200 | 0.126855331060762 | -0.108156284027933 | -0.013756547019375 | -1.856141159796850 | 0.856640489535953 |
| 96000 | 0.117298884688064 | -0.100562058095814 | -0.012539193319164 | -1.867054403682534 | 0.867478483604059 |

| rate | b0 Q30 | b1 Q30 | b2 Q30 | a1 Q30 | a2 Q30 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 44100 | 245790958 | -188270260 | -37809732 | -1859698319 | 787947107 |

The implementation uses Q30 signed coefficients, s64 accumulator, Direct
Form I, and eight fractional bits in each output history (`S24.Q8`). Inputs
remain signed S24 integers. Each sample computes the five products in Q30/Q38,
rounds to the nearest S24.Q8 state, then rounds and clamps the delivered value
to signed S24. Feed the saturated S24.Q8 value back into the recursion. Never
wrap.

The conservative worst-case sum-of-absolute-products bound, with every input
and feedback history clamped to S24.Q8, is 74.13% of signed-s64 range at the
worst rate (96 kHz), leaving 2.60 dB arithmetic headroom. This is why the state
must be bounded as well as the delivered output.

The first 10 seconds of the left channel of a local reference recording (`lin_opname.wav`, not distributed; native
44.1 kHz) were compared against a double-precision float reference. `RMS dBFS`
is the
fixed-minus-float error floor; `20-50 Hz` is windowed error energy relative to
reference signal energy in that band:

| variant | max error (S24 LSB) | RMS error (LSB) | RMS dBFS | 20-50 Hz error/signal | DC/silence behavior | nominal work/sample |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| DF-I, integer S24 state | 37.395 | 9.420 | -118.992 | -72.655 dB | 100k DC settles 126 LSB high; -127 LSB limit after returning to zero | 5 mul, 4 add/sub, 1 rounded shift |
| DF-I, S24.Q8 state | **0.650** | **0.291** | **-149.197** | **-120.635 dB** | 100k DC rounds to the ideal integer; decays exactly to zero | 5 mul, 4 add/sub, 3 rounded shifts |
| DF-I, first-order error feedback | 1.535 | 0.425 | -145.911 | -119.470 dB | DC alternates between the two nearest integers; decays exactly to zero | 5 mul, 6 add/sub, 2 rounded shifts |
| transposed DF-II, integer S24 state | 37.395 | 9.420 | -118.992 | -72.655 dB | same -127 LSB limit | 5 mul, 4 add/sub, 1 rounded shift |
| transposed DF-II, S24.Q8 state | 0.650 | 0.291 | -149.197 | -120.635 dB | decays exactly to zero | 5 mul, 4 add/sub, 3 rounded shifts |

DF-I and transposed DF-II were bit-identical in both tested precisions. TDF-II
needs two state words instead of DF-I's four histories, but DF-I makes the
bounded, saturated feedback state easier to audit. Use DF-I S24.Q8; it beats
the -90 dB low-band goal by 30 dB without noise shaping. Resampling the same
ten-second material to 48/88.2/96 kHz gives RMS errors of
0.292/0.304/0.309 LSB and 20--50 Hz error ratios of
-119.96/-113.49/-111.56 dB. Keep error feedback as a documented fallback,
not combined with Q8 state.

The earlier -54 dB low-band result used numerator coefficients that were not
actually normalized at 1 kHz (only the plotted response was normalized).
Correct 0 dB coefficients move that old integer-state result to about -73 dB,
but it still fails the -90 dB goal. This correction does not change the Q8
conclusion.

On the complete 288-second stereo file (25,401,600 sample-values), the float
filter produced **zero** values over S24 full scale. Peaks were -5.587 dBFS on
the left and -6.155 dBFS on the right. Use an explicit signed-S24 clamp anyway;
wraparound is never acceptable. No fixed input attenuation is justified by
this recording, and Windows already establishes the required 0 dB gain at
1 kHz. Preserve that level.

### Capture-code placement and cost

The insertion point is `px_cap_complete()` immediately after
`px_decode_frame()`. The refactored decode boundary makes channels 1-4
available as signed S24 integers, filters only the active
pairs, clamp, and then store them as the ALSA S32 container (`sample << 8`).
Leave digital mirror channels 5/6 untouched. Filtering before bit-serial
deserialization would be incorrect; filtering after the S32 store would add
unnecessary endian/container work.

Cost per six-channel capture frame:

- one active Phono pair: 10 s64 multiplies, 8 core add/sub operations and 6
  rounded shifts, plus clamps/state stores;
- both pairs active: 20 multiplies, 16 add/sub and 12 rounded shifts.

At 96 kHz with both pairs active that is 1.92 million multiplies/second. The
work runs in the existing capture completion context and must allocate
nothing, sleep nowhere and use preselected coefficients for the current rate.

The implemented synchronization follows the proposed lock order. The
completion already holds `px->cap.lock`, so it adds no lock acquisition in the
hot path. Source and RIAA-switch puts hold `px->mutex`, then briefly take
`px->cap.lock` to change the effective per-pair enable and reset state. Rate
selection/reset happens under the same locks before URBs start, and capture
`trigger(START)` resets state under `px->cap.lock` before setting `active`.
The module parameter `riaa=0` and every non-Phono or switched-off pair take the
bit-identical bypass path.

## 2. Codec resync at stream start

Windows sends the current six cached codec values (R7/R21/R22 for both codecs)
before every stream start. The implementation adds `codec_resync` as a boolean
parameter defaulting to `1`, rather than deleting the escape hatch. In
`px_start_streaming()` the six writes run after the initial status read and
before the SET_CUR sequence, matching the Windows trace. They reuse
`px->codec[][]`; they never substitute fixed defaults.

This is robustness, not the RIAA fix: the power-cycle test proved the existing
one-time writes were correct. The stream-start path and mixer puts serialize on
`px->mutex`, so a live put cannot be overwritten by a racing resync: a put
before start updates the shadow that resync sends; a put waiting behind start
runs after resync and wins. Each write uses the existing bounded EPIPE/timeout
retry policy. A persistent failure aborts `prepare()` instead of starting with
partially restored codec state; `codec_resync=0` remains available for an A/B
or unexpected device-specific regression.

## 3. Live mixer writes while streaming

There is no deferred `control_urb` path in the current driver. `px_codec_write()`
calls synchronous `usb_control_msg_send()`.
`px_switch_put()` calls it directly, and
`px_adc_source_put()` does the same. The codec resync runs only before stream
start under `px->mutex`, so it cannot overwrite a later live change. Existing
hardware tests while both
streams were active found every vendor-OUT `0x41` write succeeded; only
vendor-IN status reads failed (`tests/hw/REPORT.md:817-848`).

The previous apparent output-switch failure was root-caused as a stale panel
after module reload plus an ambiguous monitoring mix: an isolated amixer A/B
gave `on = audible`, `off = silent` (`tests/hw/REPORT.md:1983-1999`). Direct,
PCM and Mono control the WM8776 output/monitor mix, not the samples REAPER has
already captured; REAPER software monitoring can mask an audible Direct-path
change, and Direct/PCM are additive rather than exclusive. These observations
do not support a driver change.

Before changing this path, reproduce the new REAPER report with permanent
`dev_dbg` logging at the single `px_codec_write()` choke point and an audible
one-source-at-a-time routing test. For Capture Source, inspect newly arriving
capture samples, not an already-buffered region. Acceptance is: the `0x41`
write returns success during a continuing stream and the corresponding live
signal changes without restart. If the USB write succeeds but hardware does
not change, capture a Windows and Linux USB trace of that exact transition;
do not invent a queue/resync fix for a queue that does not exist.

## 4. `rmmod`: module reference held by the panel monitor

This is not a leaked driver reference. During this audit:

- `/sys/module/snd_usb_phasex64/refcnt` was `1`;
- every PHASE X64 PCM status was `closed`;
- PID 5681 was `alsactl monitor hw:3` with fd 6 open on
  `/dev/snd/controlC3`.

That open ALSA control handle pins the normal `THIS_MODULE` owner passed to
`snd_card_new()`. Before the fix, the panel started `alsactl monitor` with
`Popen()`, returned only the daemon thread, and discarded even that return. It
retained no process handle and registered no window-close cleanup, so
closing/reloading the panel could leave the monitor alive. That is the exact
refcount-one/no-PCM-opener signature.

The suspected driver reference paths are symmetric:

- no `try_module_get()`/`module_put()` or `usb_get_intf()`/`usb_put_intf()`
  calls exist;
- interface 1 is claimed at `phasex64.c:2296-2305` and released on probe
  failure in `px_card_free()` (`2216-2226`) or disconnect (`2399-2402`);
- the capture delayed work is synchronously cancelled in
  `px_stop_streaming()` (`878-889`) and again on disconnect (`2390-2394`);
- the MIDI hrtimer and both MIDI URBs are cancelled/killed on disconnect
  (`2395-2397`), and MIDI close paths also stop their own activity
  (`1963-1969`, `1987-1997`).

The panel fix stores the `Popen` handle, makes monitor shutdown idempotent
(`terminate`, bounded `wait`, then `kill` only if needed), and binds it to
`WM_DELETE_WINDOW` plus process exit. Its one-second refresh also notices a
dead monitor or a changed ALSA card number after device/driver reload and
starts one newly owned child. It never searches for or signals a process by
name. The automated lifecycle test covers start/stop, repeat start, card-number
reload and normal panel close.

## 5. Mainline strategy

In-kernel record-player equalization is policy DSP and would probably be
rejected for mainline even though it reproduces the vendor driver. Keep two
paths open:

1. Out-of-tree/DKMS: ship the bounded fixed-point implementation with
   `riaa=1` and the two per-pair switches, so the default matches Windows and
   `riaa=0` still exposes raw capture.
2. Mainline-oriented: the driver delivers raw samples only. Ship a PipeWire
   filter-chain configuration containing the four rate-specific RIAA biquads,
   exposed as a named Phono source, and a REAPER JSFX with the same curve for
   users who work entirely inside REAPER.

The response and fixed-point reference programs live in `tests/hw/riaa/` and
write no WAV files. No generated WAV or packet capture belongs in git.
